## The domain knowledge in the blog (raw material for the videos)

**EN 15635, simplified for India**
- Appoint a **PRSES** (Person Responsible for Storage Equipment Safety).
- **Three inspection layers:** daily/weekly visual checks (operators/supervisors) · monthly formal visual inspections (PRSES) · annual expert inspection (external competent person).
- **Damage classes:** Green = minor, monitor; Amber = moderate, unload and repair within 4 weeks; Red = severe, unload immediately, do not use until repaired.
- **Records:** location (bay/aisle/level), date, inspector, damage and class, photos, corrective action status.
- **No improper repairs:** no welding, straightening or non-OEM parts; repairs must meet the original spec.
- **Responsibility:** the user is responsible for ongoing safety after handover; OEMs for design and supply; inspectors for the quality of their inspection.
- **Stability Certificate = "a snapshot, not a shield".** Valid as of the inspection date; post-inspection damage, overloading or modification voids it.
- India has no mandatory national rack-safety standard; pressure comes from global clients, insurers, boards/ESG, and the Factories Act, 1948.

**The 5 preventable causes of collapse**
1. **Forklift impacts.** Uprights bend at the base, anchors loosen, bracing cracks. *90% of impacts go unreported in Indian warehouses (RAMS claim)*, from fear of blame and no logging system.
2. **Overloading and eccentric loading.** No updated load signs; heavy goods at the wrong levels; pallets pushed to the aisle edge at height. *A 150 mm offset on a 4.5 m rack can generate lateral moments of 20-30% of the vertical load (RAMS claim).*
3. **Undocumented or improper repairs.** Heat straightening, hammering, on-site welding, local sleeves, mixed rack brands. This can void insurance.
4. **Foundation failures.** Floor undulation, anchors loosening from micro-vibration, expansion joints, seismic micro-shocks. An upright "walking forward" over weeks.
5. **No structured, recurring inspections.** Supervisors without structural knowledge, no comparative records.

**Safe load capacity myths**
- "The beam is rated, so we're safe." Uprights may be overloaded even if beams are not.
- "We added stronger beams." That allows more weight, which can exceed the upright or base-plate capacity.
- "The top shelf is perfect for heavy pallets." It's the worst place.
- "Nothing happened last time." Stress builds over time.

**Business consequences**
- *A single rack failure can cost up to 50x the cost of proactive inspection (RAMS claim).*
- Downtime "costs lakhs per hour"; blocked aisles; emergency procurement; SLA penalties; rejected insurance claims; regulatory scrutiny; "one widely shared photo of collapsed racks".
