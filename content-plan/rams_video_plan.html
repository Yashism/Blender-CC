What that means for the films

  1. Damage is invisible and slow, so the films make it visible and fast. X-ray steel, load paths as light, months in seconds, collapses run in reverse. Live-action can only show the aftermath.

  2. Lead with the consequence, then the cause, then the fix. Buyers fear downtime and liability, not steel.

  3. Floor content is no-blame and character-led. RAMS says 90% of impacts go unreported. A felt cat can admit a hit that a real operator on camera can't, so Bolt's Night Shift carries the floor message.

  4. Compliance in India is pulled by triggers (client audits, insurers, certificate expiry, new sites), so every CTA names its trigger.

  5. Champions need something to forward: 16:9 for the boardroom, 9:16 for WhatsApp, captions always on.

  6. People buy what they can picture. Process films (what audit day looks like, what the report contains) take the risk out of the decision.

  7. Selling videos land in the buying windows: post-peak, Safety Week, FY-end, new FY, pre-monsoon, pre-peak.

04 · How we make it weekly

Build the kit once, then shoot every week

Two-week sprint, 19–30 Oct 2026

  • Parametric rack kit (real + felt materials, QR plates, load signs, guards)
  • Damage library with RAG severity (dents, twist, locks, anchors, deflection, corrosion)
  • Stress Lens heatmap driven by real statics, engineer-reviewed
  • Collapse rig, baked once, reused in weeks 1, 18 and 52
  • Twin wipe shader and RAMS UI kit; hero aisle with 3 lighting presets
  • Ollie the Owl, the RAMS inspector for Bolt's Night Shift

Weekly rhythm (two-week lead)

  1. N-2: script, storyboard, engineer sign-off, animatic
  2. N-1: layout, animation, sim, render, post (EN + Hindi captions, 16:9 + 9:16)
  3. N: publish Tuesday 10:00 IST

HERO films start 3 weeks ahead and render on Kaggle (a 60 s Cycles film takes about 18 h on the laptop).
