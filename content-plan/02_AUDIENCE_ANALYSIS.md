# Who Watches, Who Buys, and What They Need

## Market context (why now)

- **India's warehousing is at record scale.** Knight Frank puts 2025 absorption across the top 8 markets at **72.5 million sq ft, up 29% year on year**, with **Grade A at 63%** of space absorbed ([NBMCW / Knight Frank](https://www.nbmcw.com/article-report/logistics-specialized-transport/manufacturing-leads-indias-warehousing-growth-in-q3-2025-as-overall-absorption-record-16-yoy-growth-knight-frank.html), [Infrastructure Today / Colliers](https://infrastructuretoday.co.in/?p=39908)). More new sites, relocations and layout changes all create audit triggers (new installation, reused racks, layout change).
- **Structural safety in warehouses is in the news.** There were under-construction warehouse/shed collapses in Kolkata (June 2026) and at a logistics warehouse near Khalapur, Raigad (13 Oct 2026) ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1093810/scroll_in), [UNI](https://www.uniindia.com/west-india/accident-maha-warehouse-shed-collapse/212114)). These were building collapses, **not rack failures**. Never newsjack a tragedy or imply they were rack-related; the takeaway is that warehouse leaders are more alert to structural risk right now.
- **No mandatory national rack-safety standard in India** (per RAMS's EN 15635 blog). Demand is pulled in from outside: global clients' audits, insurers, certificate renewals, boards and ESG reviews, and liability under the Factories Act, 1948. **So the calendar is built around those pull moments.**

## Seven insights that shape the videos

1. **The danger is invisible and slow, so the video must make it visible and fast.** Damage builds up over weeks and nobody sees it until the collapse. This is where Blender beats live footage: X-ray steel, load paths shown as light, months compressed into seconds, time run backwards. **The creative thesis for the whole series is "show what the eye can't see".** Live-action can only show the aftermath; 3D can show the cause.
2. **Buyers fear consequences, not racks.** Lead with the consequence (downtime, a rejected claim, liability, an aisle shut in peak), then reveal the cause, then the fix. Never lead with features.
3. **Blame drives the 90%-unreported problem.** Floor content has to be no-blame. Felt animals take the blame out: a cat forklift driver can admit a mistake that a human operator in a video can't. **That's why *Bolt's Night Shift* is the right vehicle for the floor audience**, and RAMS already owns that world and its assets.
4. **Compliance is pulled, not pushed.** Nobody wakes up wanting an audit. They want one when a certificate expires, a client audits them, insurance renews, they move racks or change the layout, or after a hit. **Each video names its trigger, and its CTA names the moment.**
5. **The champion needs something to forward.** The EHS head who finds RAMS has to convince a boss and train a floor. Every video should be something they can forward: 16:9 for the boardroom, 9:16 for WhatsApp, captions on, no jargon without explanation.
6. **People buy what they can picture.** Fear of the unknown (cost, disruption, "what do we even get?") stalls deals. Process-transparency films (Inside a RAMS Rack Audit, Audit Day, Report Unboxed) take that risk out of the decision.
7. **The calendar has strong seasons.** Diwali peak (Oct-Nov), FY-end spend (March), new-FY contracts and insurance (April), monsoon (June-Sept), festive pre-stocking (Aug-Sept). The calendar times the selling videos to these windows.
