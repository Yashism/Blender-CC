What Every Warehouse Should Know About Rack Inspections
Date

Why Rack Inspections Can’t Be an Afterthought 
In modern warehouses, nearly everything is tracked and optimized, from inventory and equipment utilization to staff productivity. Yet, the very structure holding that inventory, your racking system, often receives the least attention.
Racks are assumed to be strong and silent. But over time, repeated forklift impacts, shifting loads, corrosion and unauthorized modifications silently compromise their strength. By the time the damage becomes visible or catastrophic, it is often too late.
That is why rack inspections are not just a box to check. They are the foundation of safety, efficiency and compliance.
In this blog, we explain what every warehouse needs to know: who should inspect, what to look for, how often inspections should be conducted and how RAMS makes the entire process easier and more reliable.
Why Rack Inspections Are Business-Critical
Most rack failures are not caused by one major mistake. They are caused by dozens of small oversights, such as a minor dent, an ignored anchor bolt or missing bracing that was never replaced. These failures are not just dangerous. They are also expensive.
Safety
Inspections help identify high-risk conditions before someone gets injured. Bent uprights, misaligned beams or a leaning bay may appear stable today but could collapse under load tomorrow.
Productivity
When racks fail or are taken out of operation for emergency repairs, entire aisles may need to be shut down. This slows order picking, stacking and dispatch activities. Routine inspections help prevent these unplanned interruptions.
Compliance and Legal Requirements
If a rack failure causes an injury and no inspection record is available, the warehouse operator may face action under the Factories Act, IPC or applicable labor laws. Most insurers now require documented safety audits before honoring claims.
Preventive inspections are not simply a cost. They are one of the most affordable forms of risk protection.
Consistent inspections also help identify recurring patterns. Repeated damage in certain aisles may indicate poor MHE behavior, layout flaws or overloading zones.
Over time, inspections become more than safety tools. They also serve as operational diagnostics.
How Often Should Rack Inspections Happen?
According to EN 15635, the global guideline for rack safety, inspections should be carried out at different frequencies.
Daily or Weekly Visual Checks
Warehouse staff should conduct basic checks for obvious signs of damage, such as:
Dislodged beams
Leaning racks
Fallen items
Missing components
Visible impact damage
Monthly Formal Inspections
These inspections should be conducted by the appointed PRSES, Person Responsible for Storage Equipment Safety.
They should follow a structured checklist and classify damage as Green, Amber or Red.
Annual Expert Audit
An annual expert inspection should be carried out by a competent third-party inspector, structural engineer or OEM representative.
This full-scale assessment should check:
Structural compliance
Rack integrity
Load signage
Alignment and verticality
Long-term wear and damage
Inspection frequency may vary depending on factors such as seismic zone, rack height, storage type, MHE traffic intensity and damage history.
High-density or heavy-load storage areas may require more frequent structured checks.

What Should Be Checked During an Inspection?
Rack inspection should never be treated as a simple walk-and-glance activity.
A thorough inspection should cover the following areas.
Structural Health
Check whether:
Uprights are dented, cracked or tilted
Beams are excessively deflected
Bracings are missing, loose or bent
Structural connections are damaged or incomplete
Frames are out of alignment or out of plumb
Check for splices or welds on uprights in tall racks. These modifications must be approved by the OEM or verified by a structural engineer.
Base Anchors and Floor Condition
Check whether:
Baseplates are loose, damaged or corroded
Anchor bolts are missing, loose or rusted
The concrete floor around the baseplates is damaged, spalled or uneven
Floor cracks are visible near the rack foundations
Baseplates are fully supported
These small issues can eventually lead to larger failures.
Special care must be taken in seismic zones to confirm the integrity of the anchors and rack foundations.

Compliance, Liability and Audit Readiness
Rack inspections are not just a good practice. They are central to compliance with international and national safety requirements.
EN 15635 recommends appointing a PRSES and conducting structured inspections at regular intervals
The Factories Act, 1948, holds employers responsible for maintaining the safety of workplace infrastructure
In the event of a collapse, legal and financial responsibility may rest with the warehouse operator unless inspection records and corrective actions are clearly documented
Insurance providers increasingly require proof of periodic inspections and certified structural reviews before processing warehouse-related claims
