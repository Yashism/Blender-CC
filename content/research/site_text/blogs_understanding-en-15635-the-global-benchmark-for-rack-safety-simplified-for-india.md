What Gets Standardized Gets Safer 
In Indian warehouses, rack inspections are often reactive and inconsistent. They are usually limited to visual walk around conducted without documentation or a proper understanding of structural behavior.
Modern warehouse operations demand a more systematic approach. This is where EN 15635 plays an important role. It is a globally recognize standard that brings structure, accountability and traceability to rack safety.
With increasing safety expectations from global clients and insurers, EN 15635 is becoming a benchmark for serious warehouse operators in India.
What Is EN 15635?
EN 15635 is a European standard titled “Application and Maintenance of Storage Equipment.” It provides comprehensive guidelines for:
Safe operation of industrial racking systems
Scheduled inspections
Damage classification
Repair protocols
Documentation and record keeping
This standard is advisory in Europe, but it has become the de facto global global best practice for rack maintenance. It is adopted by 3PL, FMCG and e-commerce companies worldwide.
Note
Most OEMs design racking systems using EN standards such as EN 15512 for structural design and EN 15620 for dimensional tolerances. EN 15635 ensures that these systems are operated and maintained safely after installation.
Key Principles of EN 15635 Explained
1. Appoint a PRSES, Person Responsible for Storage Equipment Safety
Every facility must formally assign a PRSES who is:
Accountable for inspections and documentation
Responsible for initiating and tracking repair actions
The point of contact during safety audits or incidents
2. Implement a Structured Inspection Frequency
EN 15635 specifies three types of inspections, each with a specific role.
Inspection Type	Frequency	Conducted By
Visual Checks	Daily or weekly	Operators or supervisors
Formal Visual Inspections	Monthly	Trained PRSES or safety officer
Expert Inspections	Annually	External competent person, such as an OEM or third-party auditor
RAMS allows the scheduling and automatic tracking of each inspection type by location, role and due date.
3. Classify Rack Damage by Severity
EN 15635 provides a three-tier classification system for damage severity.
Tag	Description	Action
🟢 Green	Minor damage, such as upright dents below 3 mm	Monitor and recheck during the next inspection
🟡 Amber	Moderate damage, such as twisting or bent braces	Unload the rack and repair it within four weeks
🔴 Red	Severe damage, such as missing bolts or significant deformation	Unload immediately and do not use until repaired
RAMS automates this classification using predefined tolerances and inspection logic based on EN 15635.
4. Maintain Structured Inspection Records
The standard emphasizes documenting everything because what is not recorded cannot be proven during insurance or compliance reviews.
Essential elements of every inspection record include:
Rack location, including bay, aisle and level
Date of inspection
Inspector’s identity
Nature and classification of damage
Photographic evidence
Corrective action status
5. Restrict Improper Repairs
EN 15635 warns against:
Welding, straightening or modifying structural elements
Using non-OEM or unverified replacement parts
Skipping engineering validation for repairs
Instead:
Repairs must meet the original specifications
Replacement parts must be verified
Follow-up inspections must validate the quality of repairs
RAMS generates a Repair Bill of Materials, or BoM, automatically mapped to the rack location, severity and cost. This makes authorized repairs easier to manage and trace.
Who Is Responsible in Case of Rack Failure?
EN 15635 makes responsibility clear.
Warehouse Operator or User
“The user of the racking system is responsible for the ongoing safety, regular inspection and maintenance of the storage equipment.”
That means:
After installation, the responsibility for safe operation lies with the operator
Failure to inspect or act on identified damage shifts legal accountability to the user
Assigning a PRSES and maintaining inspection records form part of this responsibility
Rack OEMs
OEMs are responsible for:
Supplying racks that meet EN structural design standards
Providing design drawings, capacity ratings and installation guides
Recommending safe usage parameters
After installation and handover, the OEM is no longer responsible for misuse, impact damage or neglect by the user.
Third-Party Inspectors, External Competent Persons
As per EN 15635:
The third-party inspector is responsible for providing accurate findings during the annual expert inspection
The inspector must conduct a thorough inspection, classify damage correctly and report findings honestly
However, if the warehouse fails to act on red or amber damage after it is reported, the responsibility does not shift to the inspector.
Inspectors may be held responsible if they:
Miss clear signs of damage
Fail to conduct the inspection diligently
Misrepresent findings in the inspection report
In simple terms, inspectors are responsible for the quality of their inspections, while the user remains responsible for acting on the findings.
The Real Meaning of a “Stability Certificate”
In India, it is common for third-party inspectors or OEMs to issue a Stability Certificate after an inspection.
This certificate is often used for:
Handover to clients
Insurance requirements
Compliance documentation
But what does this certificate really imply?
What It Confirms
The racks were visually inspected on a specific date
No red-level damage was found at the time of inspection
The racks were found to be plumb, anchored and apparently undamaged under normal operating conditions
The installation appeared to comply with OEM drawings and general best practices, where such references were available
What It Does Not Do
It does not guarantee future safety or act as a structural warranty
It does not shift responsibility to the inspector after handover
It does not eliminate the user’s responsibility to follow EN 15635 through ongoing inspection and maintenance
If a Collapse Happens After a Certificate Is Issued
Scenario	Who Is Responsible?
New damage occurs after the inspection	User, for failing to report the damage or arrange a re inspection
Inspector misses visible damage	Inspector, for negligence
Rack is overloaded beyond its rated capacity	User, for misuse
Certificate is issued based on incomplete data	Inspector
Rack is modified after certification	User or contractor
Stability Certificates are snapshots, not shields. Their legal value depends on how well the inspection process was documented and whether the user continued to operate the racks responsibly after the inspection.
Best Practice
Always treat the Stability Certificate as part of a wider rack safety management program, not as the closure of the safety process.
Use RAMS to attach inspection records, photographs, classification tags and BoMs
Include a disclaimer stating:
“This certificate is valid as of the date of inspection. Any post-inspection modification, damage, overloading or misalignment shall void this declaration.”
Why EN 15635 Is Crucial for India
India lacks a mandatory national standard dedicated specifically to rack safety. This often leads to:
Visual-only audits without structural logic
No maintenance tracking
Inconsistent practices across different facilities
At the same time, warehouse operators face growing pressure from:
Global clients demanding documented safety procedures
Insurance providers assessing risk based on preventive measures
Investors and boards reviewing ESG performance
EN 15635 fills a critical gap and raises the standard of rack safety management.
What Happens When You Don’t Follow EN 15635?
Ignoring structured inspections and documentation can expose an organisation to:
Rack collapses caused by unresolved damage
Injuries or fatalities that may trigger legal action under applicable factory and criminal laws
Rejection of insurance claims due to a lack of documented inspection evidence
Client contract violations, especially within global supply chains
Reputation damage, particularly when incidents become public
